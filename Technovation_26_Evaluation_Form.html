Project Title: AthleteGuard AI — Clinical Movement Screening, Limb Symmetry Index (LSI), & Post-Op Orthopedic Surgical Clearance Decision Support System
Problem & User
Which sports health problem are you solving (injury, rehab, fatigue, nutrition, performance, mental wellbeing)? Non-Contact Lower-Extremity Injuries & Post-Operative ACL Rehabilitation Clearance.
Specifically addressing acute non-contact Anterior Cruciate Ligament (ACL) tears, secondary graft re-tears, patellofemoral pain syndrome, and compensatory asymmetric overloading during jump-landing and deceleration.
What happens today without your solution, and why is that not good enough? 1. Subjective Visual Assessment: Clinicians and coaches rely on visual observation or paper LESS checklists, which suffer from >30% inter-rater variability and fail to detect millisecond dynamic valgus collapses (<50ms).
2. Prohibitive Lab Equipment Costs: Gold-standard optical 3D motion capture labs (Vicon) and force plates cost $50,000–$150,000, require physical markers, and take hours of setup.
3. Arbitrary Clearance Timelines: Orthopedic surgeons frequently clear post-ACL reconstruction patients based solely on elapsed time (e.g. "it's been 9 months"), leading to a 20% to 30% secondary re-tear rate. Without objective bilateral Limb Symmetry Index (LSI) data, early quad avoidance and compensatory contralateral overloading go undetected.
How will you measure success (e.g., injury risk detected earlier, accuracy of form correction)? 1. Objective Limb Symmetry Index (LSI ≥ 90%): Conformance with the Grindem et al. (2016) benchmark (proves that restoring ≥90% LSI reduces secondary ACL re-rupture risk by 51% per month delayed).
2. Rapid Deficit Detection: Real-time identification of frontal knee valgus collapse (FPPA >10°) and landing stiffness (<60° knee flexion) in under 60 seconds from smartphone footage.
3. Goniometric Precision: Kinematic angle accuracy within ±3° compared against manual goniometry.
Who is the primary user: athlete, coach, physiotherapist, or sports doctor? Primary: Orthopedic Surgeons, Sports Medicine Physicians, and Clinical Physical Therapists (surgical clearance, milestone tracking, and CPT-coded reimbursement).
Secondary: Head Athletic Trainers, Strength Coaches, and Competitive Athletes.
Flow Chart Available (Yes / No) Yes (Included in project dossier: Video Upload → MediaPipe 3D Tracking → Velocity Phase Parsing → Kinematic & LSI Engine → Signed CPT Clearance Certificate).
Architecture Diagram (Yes / No) Yes (Included: Streamlit Frontend → MediaPipe 33 Landmark Core → Biomechanical Engine → SQLite3 DB → ReportLab PDF / Gemini Copilot).
Data
What data do you need (wearables, video, EMG, heart rate, injury records, nutrition logs)? Standard 2D RGB Video Footage (MP4/MOV/AVI, 30–60 FPS) captured via any smartphone or tablet camera showing a full-body jump-landing or deceleration movement. Zero wearable sensors, skin markers, or calibration rigs required.
Is this data publicly available or will you generate or simulate it? Who owns it? 1. Ground Truth Benchmarks: Calibrated against validated public academic benchmarks from the Padua et al. (2009) LESS protocol and IAAF datasets.
2. Patient / Athlete Footage: Captured on-demand; strictly owned by the patient or hospital. Processed transiently in volatile application memory on-device.
Is the dataset large and balanced enough, and does it cover different ages, genders, and sports? 1. Foundation Vision Model: MediaPipe 3D Pose was trained on 25,000+ diverse global images spanning diverse ages, BMIs, genders, skin tones, and camera perspectives.
2. Multi-Sport & Clinical Calibration: Calibrated across 5 distinct archetypes (Clinical Drop Vertical Jump, Track Long Jump, Volleyball Spike, Basketball Deceleration, and Soccer Cutting).
AI Approach
Which AI technique fits (classification, time-series prediction, computer vision/pose estimation, anomaly detection, LLM-based advice)? Deep Learning 3D Computer Vision (MediaPipe BlazePose 33 Landmarks) combined with:
1. Deterministic Biomechanical Engine: Dot-product joint angle calculation, Frontal Plane Projection Angle (FPPA) deviation, and vertical ankle velocity phase segmentation.
2. Clinical Decision Support Heuristics: Weighted Limb Symmetry Index (LSI %) formulation and Padua LESS item attribution.
3. Generative Clinical AI (Google Gemini 2.5 Flash API): Synthesizes measured kinematic deficits into periodized 4-week clinical corrective exercise protocols.
What is your baseline, and how will you prove the AI adds value? Baseline: Manual observational assessment using a goniometer and paper LESS checklist (15–20 min, subjective, blind to micro-spikes).
AI Value-Add: 33 landmark points tracked across 4 phases, LSI computed, deficits flagged in <60s, and signed CPT-coded clinical clearance PDF exported automatically.
How will you validate the model (metrics, test split, expert feedback)? 1. Kinematic Precision: Benchmarking against manual goniometry across test trials (±3° tolerance).
2. Temporal Boundaries: Frame-by-frame verification of Initial Contact and Peak Flexion.
3. Expert Review: Clinical feedback by orthopedic surgeons on clearance reports and CPT 97750 / 98977 compliance.
What measurable outcome will prove that your solution works? 1. Real-time Throughput: ≥25 FPS inference on CPU.
2. 100% Detection Sensitivity: Complete capture of dynamic knee valgus collapses exceeding sport safety thresholds (>10°).
3. Clinical Stratification: Clear separation of healthy control symmetry (≥90% LSI) vs post-op deficit limbs (<80% LSI).
Safety & Ethics
How will you protect personal health data and get consent? 1. Edge Processing: Video analyzed transiently in volatile RAM without third-party cloud storage.
2. De-Identification: Anonymized Patient MRN / Athlete IDs.
3. User Control: Built-in "Reset Database & Clear History" button for instant local database purge.
What are the risks of a wrong prediction, and how will you show uncertainty? Risks: Premature return-to-play clearance leading to graft rupture, or false-positive holds delaying an athlete's career.
Uncertainty Mitigation: Confidence thresholding (rejects <0.5 confidence points), camera perspective detection (flags lateral projection of valgus), and 3-tier risk classification with transparent biomechanical attribution.
How will you make clear this is decision support and not a medical diagnosis? 1. Ethical Disclaimers: Prominently displayed across all UI screens and PDFs: "Clinical decision support tool, not an autonomous medical diagnosis device. Final surgical clearance remains under the judgment of the attending physician."
2. Physician Sign-off: Clearance certificates mandate physical surgeon signature and state medical license number before EMR submission.
